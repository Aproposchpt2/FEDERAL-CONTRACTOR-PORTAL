Community Business Development · Federal Contractor Pathway

Your client registered to compete. Help them move from registration to relevant opportunity.

Business Development Centers and government-contracting advisors already help businesses understand registration, certifications, capability development, market research, and federal procurement. RFCP can extend that work for clients who are already registered federal contractors by organizing federal opportunity review around the contractor's business profile instead of requiring the contractor to repeatedly search the entire marketplace.

The institutional role: Your organization continues providing counseling, education, technical assistance, and professional judgment. RFCP provides a personalized federal opportunity-intelligence layer for registered contractor clients who need a more focused path from registration into opportunity review.

Registration creates access. It does not eliminate the search burden.

A registered federal contractor still has to identify which federal notices are relevant, understand how NAICS and set-aside signals relate to the business, monitor timing and geography, review the solicitation package, and decide whether an opportunity deserves pursuit resources.

RFCP is designed around that post-registration problem. It uses contractor-profile information and SAM-derived NAICS signals to prioritize public federal opportunities for review. The objective is not to reproduce the entire federal opportunity universe for each contractor. The objective is to help the contractor focus attention on opportunities with stronger business alignment.

Where RFCP can extend Business Development Center services

After SAM registration

Give registered contractor clients a next-step pathway after the registration milestone instead of leaving them with a broad instruction to keep searching SAM.gov.

After capability development

Use the contractor's business profile and procurement signals to organize discovery around what the business actually provides.

During opportunity review

Prioritize public federal opportunities before the contractor invests substantial time reading every available notice.

Before pursuit

Move from a matched opportunity into solicitation-specific review and, when appropriate, deeper Analyze Fit decision support.

A practical referral model for advisors

  1. Confirm the client is a registered federal contractor. RFCP is specifically designed for that audience.
  2. Continue the advisor-led contracting conversation. Registration, capability, certifications, readiness, and strategy remain important.
  3. Refer the client into RFCP for personalized opportunity prioritization. The contractor reviews opportunities ranked around the business profile.
  4. Use the official solicitation as controlling authority. SAM.gov and the issuing agency remain the official source for requirements, amendments, deadlines, and eligibility conditions.
  5. Support the pursuit decision. A match is a reason to review—not a determination that the contractor should bid.

What RFCP does not replace

Your advisor relationship

RFCP does not replace business counselors, APEX professionals, SBDC advisors, procurement specialists, attorneys, accountants, bonding specialists, or other human experts.

SAM.gov

SAM.gov remains the official federal source for public opportunity notices and controlling solicitation information.

Eligibility verification

A match does not establish size status, set-aside eligibility, licensing, experience, responsiveness, or compliance with mandatory requirements.

The bid/no-bid decision

The contractor remains responsible for determining whether pursuit is commercially, technically, financially, and operationally appropriate.

Use RFCP for the right client at the right stage

Best fit: a business that has already completed federal registration and needs a more personalized way to identify and evaluate federal contract opportunities.

Earlier-stage client: if the business still needs broader business readiness, planning, or funding guidance, the National Enterprise Business Center institutional pathway may be the more appropriate starting point.
